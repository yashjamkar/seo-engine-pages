Clicks, impressions, CTR and position

Search Console's performance report counts how a site appears in Google Search. The four numbers are simple; the rules behind them are where people get caught out.

The four numbers

NumberWhat it counts
ImpressionsHow often a link to the site appeared in results someone saw.
ClicksHow often someone clicked through to the site.
Click-through rateClicks divided by impressions.
PositionThe average position of the site's highest result, where 1 is the top.

Quirks worth knowing

  • Delay. Data arrives after a delay of a couple of days. The most recent days are preliminary and can still go up once they are final.
  • Pacific time. Days are counted in US Pacific time, not your local time zone, so a "day" may not match your own analytics.
  • Hidden queries. Rare searches are left out of the query list to protect searchers' privacy, but still count in page and site totals. Query rows therefore add up to less than the total.
  • Row limits. Very large sites see only the top rows for a day; totals are still complete.
  • History. About sixteen months of data are kept.

Reading the numbers together

Clicks and impressions can move for different reasons. When impressions rise but the click-through rate falls and clicks stay flat, the page is often being shown in more places that people scroll past, such as summaries at the top of the results. That is not necessarily a problem with the page itself.
