Measuring whether a change worked

"We changed the title and clicks went up 12%" sounds like proof. Usually it is not, because clicks would have moved anyway.

Why before-and-after misleads

  • Weekly and seasonal cycles. Many sites are quieter at weekends and busier before holidays. Compare the wrong weeks and you measure the calendar.
  • Search engine updates. A ranking update can lift or drop many sites at once, unrelated to your change.
  • Noise. Small pages swing by double digits from week to week by chance.

A fairer comparison

Compare the changed page with similar pages that were not changed: pages built from the same template, with similar traffic. If the changed page rose and its comparison pages did not, the change is the likely cause. This is called a difference-in-differences comparison.

Report the result as a range, not a single number, for example "+4% to +15%", and wait until the range is narrow enough to decide. When there are too few comparable pages, the honest answer is that the effect cannot be measured yet.

Before you change anything

Check whether the page is really underperforming. A forecast built from its own history and from similar pages tells you whether this week's dip is unusual or just normal variation.
