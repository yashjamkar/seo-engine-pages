Page titles in search results

The clickable headline of a search result usually comes from the page's <title> element, but not always, and not always in full.

Where the title comes from

Google builds the title link mainly from the page's <title>. It may also use the main heading, other prominent text on the page, or the text of links pointing to it, especially when the written title is missing, repeated across many pages, stuffed with keywords, or does not describe the page.

What the visitor actually sees

The text people see is often not the text you typed. Two common reasons:

  • Templates. Content management systems and SEO plugins often add a site name or separator, for example Pricing | Example Shop. Judge a title on the rendered page, not on the field you edited.
  • Truncation. Long titles are cut to fit the width of the result and end in an ellipsis. The limit is set by display width, not a fixed number of characters, so wide letters run out sooner.

Writing a good one

  • Say what the page is, specifically, near the start.
  • Give every page its own title; duplicates are a common reason for rewrites.
  • Keep a brand suffix short, or leave it off long titles.
  • Check the rendered result after a change: the page source, then the live search result once the page has been crawled again.
